Food safety

How long it keeps

WhatFridgeFreezer, vacuum-sealed
Steaks, chops and roasts3–5 daysUp to 6 months
Ground meat and poultry1–2 daysUp to 6 months

Keep your fridge at 40°F or below and your freezer at 0°F. Store raw meat on the bottom shelf so nothing drips onto food you won’t cook.

Thawing

  • In the fridge. The safest way. Steaks and chops usually thaw overnight. A big roast can take a day or more. Meat thawed in the fridge can go back in the freezer if your plans change.
  • In cold water. Faster. Keep the meat in its sealed package, put it under cold tap water, and change the water every 30 minutes. Cook it as soon as it has thawed.
  • Not on the counter. The outside warms up past 40°F while the middle is still frozen, and that’s the range where bacteria grow fast.

USDA safe minimum internal temperatures

WhatSafe minimum
Beef, pork and lamb steaks, chops and roasts145°F, then rest 3 minutes
Ground beef, pork and lamb, including fresh sausage160°F
Poultry, whole, in pieces or ground165°F

Check with an instant-read thermometer in the thickest part, away from the bone.

Pull temperatures on our product pages

Our product pages list chef-style pull temperatures. For whole cuts of beef, pork and lamb they’re lower than the USDA numbers, because you take the meat off the heat early and it keeps cooking while it rests. For poultry and ground meat they’re the USDA numbers or higher.

The USDA numbers above are the safe minimums. For ground meat and poultry, go by the USDA numbers. If you’re cooking for someone who is pregnant, older, very young or has a weakened immune system, use them for everything.
