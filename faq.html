Common questions

Why is the price an estimate?

Because we don’t know the exact weight until we cut it. Every animal is a little different, so a ribeye from one steer won’t weigh the same as a ribeye from the next. Each item shows an approximate weight and an estimated price based on it.

What does “charged by actual weight” mean?

We price meat per pound and charge for what’s on the scale after we cut your order. If your chops come in a bit heavy, you pay a bit more. If they come in light, you pay less. It’s usually within 10% of the estimate, and the charge is capped at the estimate plus 10%. If a piece comes in heavier than that, the extra is on us.

A few things are priced per piece instead: stock by the quart, tallow by the jar, and gift cards.

Why is something sold out?

We buy whole animals, and each one only has so much of each cut. A steer has one hanger steak. When a cut sells out, it’s out until the next animal comes in. The board shows what’s left this week.

Can you cut it thicker?

Usually, if we still have that part of the animal in one piece. Write the thickness you want under “Add a note for the counter” on the product page, or call before you order. If we can’t do it, we call you before we cut. A thicker steak weighs more, so the price goes up with it.

How do I cancel the Butcher’s Box?

In your Account, by email or by phone. Boxes are cut on Wednesdays. Skip or cancel any time before Sunday 11:59 PM Eastern and that week’s box won’t be cut or charged. There’s no cancellation fee.

What’s in the butcher’s pick?

A fixed list of cuts for each box size, with a note from Marisol. You can see the whole list before you order, or pick your own 6, 10 or 14 cuts.

Can I freeze it?

Yes. Vacuum-sealed, it keeps up to 6 months in the freezer. Thaw it in the fridge, not on the counter. Fresh, steaks and roasts keep 3–5 days in the fridge, and ground meat and poultry 1–2 days. More on the food safety page.

Do you ship to Alaska or Hawaii?

No. Overnight shipping covers the lower 48 states only, and we don’t ship to US territories or outside the US. The delivery page explains why.

Can you get me something you don’t stock?

Often, yes. Duck, rabbit, goat, quail, guinea hen and farm-raised venison usually come in within a week. A whole suckling pig needs 10 days’ notice.

Venison is farm-raised only, because wild game can’t legally be sold in the US.
